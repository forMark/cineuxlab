Case Study · 001

Reducing friction
in movie discovery.

How information hierarchy can help people move from curiosity to a confident movie choice.

Area Movie discovery
Study UX / Information Architecture
Status Exploratory
01

The problem

Movie pages tend to accumulate information over time. Ratings, genres, cast, reviews, trailers, recommendations and editorial content all compete for the same attention.

The result is often a page that is comprehensive, but difficult to scan when someone simply wants to answer one question: “Is this worth watching?”

02

The hypothesis

The first screen should support a decision, not explain the entire movie.

A useful discovery experience can progressively reveal detail instead of presenting every available signal simultaneously.

03

What users need first

01 Context

What kind of movie is this?

02 Signal

Do other people consider it worth watching?

03 Confidence

Does it match what I want right now?

04

Design direction

Instead of treating every piece of metadata equally, the interface can establish a clear hierarchy:

Primary Title, genre, premise, rating
Secondary Reviews, cast, runtime, release information
Exploration Similar films, collections and recommendations
05

Key insight

Good discovery UX doesn't reduce the amount of information.
It reduces the amount of information users have to process at once.

06

Takeaways

  • Prioritize decision-critical information.
  • Use progressive disclosure for deeper metadata.
  • Separate discovery from exploration.
  • Let reviews support a decision rather than dominate it.

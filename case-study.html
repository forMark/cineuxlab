Case Study · 001

Reducing friction
in movie discovery.

Exploring how information architecture can help people move from curiosity to a confident movie choice.

Focus

Movie discovery interfaces

Problem

Information overload during film selection

Goal

Make important signals easier to understand

The problem

Movie pages often contain everything a user could want to know — but not necessarily in the order they need it.

Posters, ratings, genres, reviews, trailers and recommendations compete for attention. The result can be a page that is rich in information but difficult to scan.

Research questions

01

Which information helps users decide whether a movie is worth exploring?

02

How should ratings and reviews fit into the discovery flow?

03

What can be removed without reducing confidence?

Key insight

The goal is not to show more information. It is to reveal the right information at the right moment.

UX principles

Hierarchy

Prioritize the signals users need before secondary metadata.

Progressive disclosure

Let deeper information appear when users are ready for it.

Decision support

Help users compare options without forcing them to process everything at once.

Conclusion

Good movie discovery UX turns a large amount of content into a clear path toward a decision.
